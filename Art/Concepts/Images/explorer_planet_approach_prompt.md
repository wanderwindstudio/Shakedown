## Prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Generate ONE landscape 16:9 image showing what a player experiences travelling through space and APPROACHING AN UNEXPLORED PLANET in Shakedown, a calm first-person indie space exploration game. Use the supplied cockpit image as a design/material reference for the same ship, but create a NEW composition from the SEATED PILOT'S FIRST-PERSON EYE POSITION looking out through the windshield, not from behind the chair. No chair back in foreground, no visible player body. The view outside is the main subject, occupying around 70 percent of the frame: an immense beautiful alien ocean planet ahead, a clearly curved sphere with much of its circular limb visible against space, not already inside atmosphere. Planet appears close and growing in the windshield, filling most of the central view yet clearly a world ahead at orbital approach distance. Teal oceans, a few irregular ochre/sage island chains and sweeping pale cream cloud systems painted as translucent watercolor washes; a delicate cyan atmospheric rim, sunlit crescent and soft violet terminator suggesting vastness. The distant sun just beyond upper-left windshield creates restrained warm light along clouds and a cool reflected glow inside. Sparse small stars in deep desaturated indigo, one tiny distant moon, no cluttered nebula wallpaper, no asteroid storm, no warp speed streaks. The lower quarter of frame shows the simple chunky matte sage-and-cream horseshoe console with a few tactile controls and three compact embedded screens: quiet abstract star map, ship schematic and a small globe with an understated circled destination on the navigation screen. No floating HUD over planet, no text or numbers. Thick charcoal angular window mullions frame the view at sides and above, slim upper utility conduit, a hint of amber task light, secured ceramic mug at lower-right conveys a little travelling home. Strong sense of calm forward travel and discovery, cosy shelter versus immense space. Achievable low-poly indie 3D game aesthetic: simple visibly modeled faceted cockpit geometry with hand-painted WATERCOLOUR textures, soft pigment blooms, paper grain, subtle uneven painted wear, quiet flat-shaded materials. Planet also visibly painterly, stylized physically round form with watercolor land/cloud textures, not a NASA photograph. Clear 3D depth, restrained lighting, no photoreal AAA detail, no dense greebles, no glossy spaceship chrome. No combat, weapons, people, captions, logos, watermark, border. This is a concept of space approach, not atmospheric entry or landing. Wonder and anticipation are the emotional focus.
